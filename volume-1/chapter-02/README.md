# Volume I · Chapter 2 — Lab: AI Timeline Analysis

**Type: Paper exercise.** No code, no setup.

## What You Will Build

A timeline of the developments that produced modern foundation models, annotated with
what each one made possible that was not possible before.

## Learning Outcomes

- Place the major eras of AI research in order and explain what ended each one.
- Explain what caused the AI winters in terms of unmet expectations rather than dates.
- Identify which developments were conceptual and which were driven by compute or data.
- Explain why the transformer displaced recurrent architectures.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 2 read in full
- **Estimated API cost: $0.00** — no code, no model calls

## Setup

None.

## Running It

Build a timeline of at least twelve entries from symbolic AI to the present. For each:

| Year | Development | What it made possible | What still blocked progress |
|------|-------------|-----------------------|-----------------------------|

Then mark each entry as **conceptual** (a new idea) or **enabling** (compute, data, or
engineering that let an existing idea finally work).

## Expected Result

A completed timeline plus roughly 400 words answering:

1. How many of your entries are enabling rather than conceptual? What does that ratio
   suggest about where the next advance comes from?
2. Both AI winters followed periods of confident prediction. What was promised, and what
   was actually delivered?
3. Attention was published years before it dominated. Why the delay?

## Verifying Your Work

No automated check. Your timeline is adequate if the "what still blocked progress"
column of each row explains why the next row exists.

If that column is empty anywhere, you have listed an event rather than explained a cause.

## If It Does Not Work

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Timeline is a list of model releases | Recency bias | At least half your entries should predate 2017 |
| Cannot fill "what still blocked progress" | Treating history as inevitable | Ask what practitioners at the time complained about |
| Everything looks conceptual | Underrating engineering | Consider GPU availability, dataset scale, and training cost |

## Going Further

Write the entry you expect a 2035 edition of this book to add for 2026. State what it
makes possible and what it leaves blocked. Keep it; check it later.
